# Front end

[Documentation](../README.md) › [Architecture](README.md)

The front end has no build step and no client-side state. Pages are rendered on the server by Phlex, Turbo moves between them and updates parts of them, and Stimulus controllers add the behaviour HTML can't. It is built for a phone first, and installs as a PWA.

## Phlex

Views are Ruby classes, in two directories. `app/components` is the kit: pieces that know no area (`Components::Modal`, `Components::ItemAction`, `Components::StatusBadge`), take plain values or a record handed to them, and never name one area's routes, policies or views. `app/views/<area>/` is everything of one area: its pages and modals (`Views::Books::Show`, `Views::Tasks::Modals::Form`) and the cards, rows, badges and form sections they are built from (`Views::Calendar::Cards::TaskCard`, `Views::Conversations::Bubble`), whoever renders them, a controller, a broadcaster or another area's view. A piece is the kit's when it would still make sense with its area deleted. What draws nothing, such as a notification's words or a figure's format, is a presenter in `app/presenters`. The same component renders a page, a Turbo Stream answer, or a broadcast to someone else.

- **Every word is `t()`,** in English and Italian. A test fails on a missing or unused key.
- **Every number, amount and date** is formatted in the reader's language and currency.
- **An error page is in the reader's language too.** It is served by `ErrorPages`, which reads only the request, never the database, which may be what failed: the language the person's last page was in, kept in their session by `ApplicationController`, else the browser's, else English. The page that turns away an old browser is written the same way, and picked by the person's language.
- **No inline event handlers:** Phlex refuses them, and behaviour goes in a Stimulus controller.
- **Text is text.** Nothing is marked `html_safe`, and a lint rule enforces it. Markup only comes from a helper that builds it.
- **A view never runs a query: whoever renders it hands it what it shows.** The controller loads the page's records through the policy's scope and passes them in; the bar on every page gets its counts the same way (`Navbar::Facts`). The one exception is a picker: a form's options come from a lookup the model names (`Unit.names_for(profile)`, `Collection.choices_for`), since every form that offers them would otherwise load the same list in its controller. Even there a view never writes a `where`, an `order` or SQL: how records are found is their model's business.

## Where actions go

**Every button sits on the thing it acts on:**

| Acts on | Goes | Component |
|---|---|---|
| One item: a card, a row, a line of the assistant's card | On that item, a round icon button | `Components::ItemAction` |
| More than two actions on one item | The rest behind one "more" button, as a labelled menu, the destructive ones last | `Components::ItemMenu` |
| Everything in a card or modal (Save, Apply, Undo) | The footer | `modal__btn` |
| The whole page (new, search, edit this record) | The top bar: two round buttons beside the bell, the search counting as one, the rest behind "more" with deleting last | `Components::PageControls` |

**A menu that every item of a long page would repeat is drawn once for the page**: the calendar's cards share one "more" menu and its days one menu of the kinds, and a conversation's messages one picker of quick emoji. The item's button (`Components::MenuButton`) says which item it is, and the menu's controller fills the menu for it and opens it beside the button. It looks and asks as an item's own menu does.

**A menu behind a button is a disclosure, not an ARIA menu.** Its button says whether it is open (`aria-expanded`) and nothing more; its entries are ordinary links and buttons, reached with Tab after the button, as everywhere else on the page. An ARIA menu promises arrow keys and type-ahead, which these short lists don't need. A menu closes when the focus leaves it, and on Escape or a chosen entry it gives the focus back to its button, so whatever the entry opened can give it back there in turn.

Each of them draws a `Components::Action`, one value however it is drawn, and draws it through one element: a link for what opens or leads somewhere, a link Turbo sends with its method for a press that changes something (so it works inside a form too), and a form posted outside Turbo for a download. An icon button always carries a label, which becomes its `aria-label` and tooltip.

**A press that needs a yes asks in the app's own dialog,** never the browser's `confirm()`. The action is given `confirm:` with the question, and Turbo hands the question to `Components::ConfirmDialog`, which the layout renders once. Its confirming button takes the press's own label ("Delete book", "Unlink from Sam"), and it is set apart in red when the press deletes. Cancel has the focus, so a stray Enter never confirms. A script asks the same dialog through a Stimulus outlet, as the uploader does before closing. Every full page names itself: its view's `page_title` is the browser tab's, its `Components::PageHeader` shows the same name once at the top of the page, with the way back to its parent page when it has one, and its `navbar_controls` are the top bar's controls (`Views::Base`). The top bar itself carries the wordmark, never the page's name. The controller hands the view to `render_page`, which reads both before rendering it.

## Navigation

The chrome is one component on every signed-in page: a top bar, the navigation, and on phones a bottom bar. The navigation is one list of links (the everyday pages, the household's sections with their pages folded under them, its people, the admin's pages) drawn once. On a phone it is a drawer pulled up from the bottom by the Menu tab and pulled back down by its handle; from a tablet up it is a panel slid in from the left by the top bar's burger, with the wordmark at its head, over a page that keeps its whole width. Nothing in the markup changes between the two: the stylesheet decides, and one script opens and closes both.

For someone using a keyboard, the first stop on every signed-in page is "Skip to content", shown only while it has the focus, which jumps past the chrome into the page's `main#content`. The top bar is a `header`, the page's banner, and its parts are written in the order they are seen, left to right, because that is the order Tab takes them in. A page for someone signed out has its own `main` too. The drawer is inert while it is closed, since it is only moved out of sight, and so is a folded group's list of links; while it is open, everything else is, so the focus stays in the drawer until it closes and goes back to whatever opened it.

Every link that leads to a page, in the navigation, an area's tabs or a row of filter pills, is one `Components::NavItem`: where it goes, its words, and the icon, count or section colour it has. The phone's bottom bar and the navigation draw the everyday pages from the same list.

## Modals and forms

A record is created and edited in a modal (`Components::Modal`). A controller names each move with the `Modals` concern's helpers, each a Turbo Stream:
- **Opening:** `render_modal` puts it in `#modals` (`open_modal` when it answers a press), in place of any modal already open. One modal shows at a time, so a list whose rows open forms of their own (a supply's purchases) is a page, not a modal. Each modal names the page it belongs on, and its address opened as a page (in a new tab, or by signing in again) goes there instead of showing the stream that opens it.
- **Errors:** `redraw_modal` brings the same form back with its errors, with a 422. They are listed above the fields in `Components::ErrorSummary`, a critical notice that stays until the form is sent again and is announced as an alert, never as toasts that close on their own. `Forms::Errors` words them: an error on a row of one of the form's lists names the list and the row ("Ingredients, row 2: Quantity must be greater than 0"), and every field is named as the form's label names it. The pages before sign-in and the settings pages list theirs the same way. A form opened on something a housemate saved since comes back the same way, saying who, with what was typed in it over what they saved. Each error is also said under the field or the row it is about, which is marked, and the control points to it (`aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby`), so a screen reader hears what is wrong where it stands.
- **Success:** a 303 back to the page the modal was opened on (`RedirectsAfterWrites`), with the toast in the flash. Turbo morphs that page in place and keeps the reader's scroll, so its title, its order, its counts and its empty states are drawn again from the records, and the modal closes with it. A delete made on the deleted record's own page goes to its list instead. A list worked through in place (a grocery list's quick-add bar and ticks) answers with its own regions instead, so the field being typed into is never drawn again.
- **Refused:** the reason as a toast where the person stands, with a 422 (`ApplicationController#refuse`): the ingredient is still on a list, the ISBN can't be read, the request was answered already. A record a housemate deleted a moment ago, or a form whose token no longer matches the session, is answered the same way, with a 404 or a 422, and the page is brought up to date under the toast; only a page opened directly, with no page to stay on, gets the public page for its status. A toast's kind says what happened: success for what is done, notice for what has begun or is held, alert for a refusal or a failure. A screen reader hears every toast as it comes: the toasts' container is a polite live region, and an alert is an `alert` of its own, read at once. A success or a notice goes by itself after a few seconds, waiting while the pointer is over it or the focus is in it; an alert stays until it is closed, since a refusal that goes by itself can go before it is read.
- **Failed:** a press that gets no answer, because the connection is gone, or a crash's 5xx, is told by the browser, as a toast where the person stands, and the page, the modal and what was typed stay (`failed_request_controller.js` on `Components::Toasts`, the layout's toasts, which also hold the words). So is a part of a page in a frame whose answer doesn't hold it, which keeps what it showed.

A modal is the browser's own modal dialog, so it works by keyboard and screen reader as the browser's dialogs do. It takes the focus when it opens: the field its form marks autofocus, else, with a mouse and keyboard, its first field (the one the server refused, when a form comes back), and on a touch screen the dialog itself, so no keyboard rises over a field nobody tapped. The focus stays in it, Escape and the back gesture close it, and on closing the focus goes back to what opened it, across a modal drawn again with its errors. The page behind can't be pressed or read aloud while it is open, so the toasts shown meanwhile show inside the modal.

Only the modal's body scrolls, and its footer always shows, which a test checks on every modal the suite renders. Every modal has one shape, short or long: on a phone a sheet that rises from the bottom edge, its footer under the thumb, and from a tablet up a dialog hanging from the same line under the top bar. The confirm dialog takes the same shape.

`Components::Form` wraps a record's form. Its Save can always be pressed, so a keyboard or a screen reader always finds it: with a required field still missing, it sends nothing, says under each missing field that it is required, and takes the focus to the first (`modal_form_controller.js`). Nothing is marked before the reader has done anything: a field says it is missing once it was filled in and left empty, or once Save was pressed, and a form the server sent back refused says so of every missing field from the start. The line under the field is what the control's `aria-describedby` names while it shows, and the control is `aria-invalid`. `Views::Occurrences::DayForm` is the form for a day of a series, rendered by `Views::Occurrences::Form`, the base every kind's form inherits. It is handed the viewer and adds "This day only / The whole series", and the read-only and "ask" states a linked housemate's item can have (`Occurrences::Access`).

**Every field is a `Components::Field`**: its label, its control inside Bulma's `.control`, its errors and its help. A record's field goes through `form_field(f, :name, label:)` in a view, which ties the label to the control's id, marks it required when the record validates the attribute's presence or its column can't be empty and has no default, and hands it what the record refused in it; the field hands its block what the control carries then (`field.aria`). Rows a form adds one at a time (a lab result's values, a recipe's steps) are drawn by `Components::NestedFields`, which gives each row its hidden id and `_destroy` inputs and `Components::ItemAction`'s remove button, says under a row what was refused in it (`errors:`, from `Forms::Errors#rows`), and puts `Components::AddRowButton` under them; `nested_fields_controller.js` adds a row from the template and takes one away. **Every field is named by its label**, tied by `for` and never by a placeholder. A group of fields (the week's days, a cost section, a list of rows) is a `fieldset` named by its `legend`. A system test opens every form a route draws and runs axe on it.

## Report pages

A page about a long job or an area's configuration (the dictionaries' imports, the assistant's eval runs, its settings) is built from shared pieces: `Components::Report::StatusCard`, then `Components::Disclosure` for the parts that fold and `Components::Panel` for those that always show, with a `Components::Notice` above them when the reader must act:
- **One status card at the top** holds everything about the job itself: its status (an icon and a word), when it started, a segment per step, what it is doing now or how it ended.
- **Then sections that state their result in their header,** so the page reads without opening anything. A section that failed, is running or waits for the reader starts open, and a healthy one starts closed.
- **On a page that polls while a job runs,** the reader's choice to open or close a section survives each reload.

## Turbo

The layout turns on page refreshes (`turbo-refresh-method=morph`, `turbo-refresh-scroll=preserve`). The rules that go with them:

1. **A control that revisits the page** (a filter, a sort, a period, a month) is a `replace` visit to the same path. Turbo morphs the page and keeps the reader's scroll. A plain link would scroll to the top.
2. **A `turbo-frame` with a `src`** carries `refresh: :morph`, so a page refresh reloads it in place instead of leaving it on its placeholder.
3. **A Stimulus controller survives a morph:** what it derives from its values it also recomputes in `<name>ValueChanged`, because `connect()` doesn't run again.
4. **A list edited in place** adds or removes one row, and never rebuilds the rows the person already set.
5. **A Turbo Stream replaces what changed, no more.**
6. **The focus survives a redraw.** Whatever is pressed and then drawn again by its answer or a broadcast (a day's circle, its title) keeps the same id, so Turbo gives the focus back to it, a modal drawn again keeps it where it was, and a modal closing gives it to its opener drawn again. A form's button takes it back once its answer is in, though Turbo switched the button off while sending. A row that leaves its list names where the focus goes instead (the next row, the one before, the list's heading), and a focus with nowhere else to go lands on the page's content, never back at the top bar (`focus_keeper_controller.js` on the body).
7. **No page is shown from memory.** Turbo keeps no snapshot of a page (`turbo-cache-control: no-cache`), so Back asks for the page as it is now. A page the browser itself kept (its back-forward cache) and a page whose live connection dropped and came back, as after a phone slept, refresh themselves (`catch_up_controller.js`). That refresh, and one the server pushes when many things changed at once (`Calendar::Refresh`) or a list is ordered as each reader has it (`Books::Broadcaster`), keeps an open modal, the toasts and what the reader typed or opened. Only a refresh Turbo carries out keeps them: Turbo leaves out the echo of a change made from the page itself, and a refresh that arrives while a visit is on its way, so the page that answers the reader's own press or click still brings its toast and closes its modal.

## Stimulus

There are about 40 controllers, one behaviour each (`uploader_controller.js`, `chart_controller.js`, `barcode_scanner_controller.js`, `dropdown_controller.js`…).
- **Words and markup come from the server:** words as values, markup as a `<template>` the view renders. A controller never writes copy.
- **A request a controller sends itself** carries the page's CSRF token, through one shared helper.
- **A phrase a controller completes** ("%{percent}% sent") is filled by one shared helper, so the words stay in the locale files.
- **A menu drawn once for many buttons** is placed, opened and closed by one shared module (`helpers/floating_menu.js`); each such menu's controller only fills it for the button pressed (`card_menu_controller.js`, `day_add_controller.js`, `reaction_picker_controller.js`).
- **What needs no DOM is a plain module**, under `app/javascript/helpers` when shared and beside its controller otherwise (the uploader's queue, memory and wording in `app/javascript/uploader`), with unit tests under `test/javascript` that Node's own runner runs (`npm test`).
- **A controller with logic of its own is unit-tested too**, on jsdom, with the Stimulus and Turbo the gems serve (`test/javascript/controllers/`): the forms' controllers (a dose's times, a payment's fields, nested rows, the recurrence fields), the conversation's, and the catch-up refresh's. A controller that only wires the page to a module or to Turbo is left to the system tests.
- **`disconnect()` cleans up everything:** listeners, timers, observers, the camera.
- **A library is the business of one controller.** Chart.js belongs to `chart_controller.js` alone. The barcode library and its WebAssembly belong to `barcode_scanner_controller.js` alone, loaded with a dynamic `import()` only when the scanner opens, and vendored rather than fetched from a CDN.

## Style

- **Bulma, in parts:** its dark theme alone, its base, elements, form and layout, and none of its components, grid or helpers. The app's own BEM blocks do everything else, and the one helper the views use, `.is-sr-only`, is the app's own. A block is never named after a Bulma component that is loaded.
- **A dark theme from tokens.** Every colour, shadow, `z-index`, font size, corner radius and transition comes from `_variables.scss`, and a colour token is named for its role (a surface's step, a text's weight, the accent, a status, a section), never its look (`$motion-quick` for a colour on hover, `$motion-settle` for something that moves or folds), and translucency comes from a fixed scale (`tint($color, "subtle")`; a bar floating over content is `glass()`, the backdrop of a dialog or the drawer `$scrim`), never a hand-picked alpha. Stylelint holds it: outside `_variables.scss` a hex, a named colour or a colour function (`rgba(`, `color-mix(`) fails the lint. The palette's contrast is measured rather than written down: a test reads the variables and fails when a text colour falls under AA on a surface it may sit on, or a chart mark under 3:1 on the chart surface.
- **Only the icons the app uses are downloaded.** Font Awesome Free is kept whole in `vendor/fontawesome`, and what the browser gets is cut from it by `bin/rails icons:subset`: a stylesheet holding only the icons the code names, and fonts holding only their glyphs (`FontAwesome::Subset`). A test fails when the code names an icon the cut lacks, which would otherwise show as a blank square.
- **The same colours outside the stylesheet.** The colour the browser paints its bars with, the icons in `public/` and the error pages served from `public/` take their hex from `Theme`, which a test holds to `_variables.scss`. The error pages are rendered by `Views::PublicPages::Error` and written by `bin/rails public:pages`, one file per language (`404.html` in English, which Rails falls back to, and `404.it.html`); a test fails when the files fall behind the view.
- **Three page widths.** A page's root block takes `page-width("column")`, `("grid")` or `("wide")` from `_variables.scss`: what is read top to bottom stays a column, a list of cards spreads to a row of them, and a dashboard or a console takes the room its tables need. No page picks a width of its own.
- **Four breakpoints.** A layout changes only at one of the four named widths in `_variables.scss` (`@include from("tablet")`, `@include until("tablet")`); a script that watches the same width reads it from the `--breakpoint-*` custom properties on `:root` rather than keep a copy.
- **Pink means "act" or "you are here",** never decoration. The four sections whose items sit on the calendar, and the assistant, each have a hue, read through `--section-color`; the other sections take the neutral.
- **Status is never colour alone:** a status always has an icon or a word too. A status pill is `Components::StatusBadge` on any page, and each kind of status writes its tone and icon side by side once, as a table of `Components::StatusLook`s that its pills, its marks and its status cards all read, a count on a link or an icon (unread messages, the bell) is `Components::CountBadge`, and a chart, table or list with nothing to show is one `Components::EmptyState` line.
- **Contrast holds in every state:** a state is shown with a surface, a border or an icon, never with `opacity` on text.
- **Variants and states are named apart:** a variant from the data is a `--modifier` (`.task-card--completed`), a state set by a script or the request is `is-*` (`is-open`, `is-current`).

## Accessibility

The target is WCAG 2.2 AA. axe runs on every page a system test visits, and on every form. On top of what axe checks:
- Anything that can be pressed is at least `$target-min` (24px) square. A control drawn smaller, such as a calendar card's status circle, keeps its look and reaches that size through `hit-area()`, an invisible layer of its own; axe checks the boxes it can measure.
- Every control shows that it has the focus, and what the browser scrolls to stops clear of the fixed bars: `html` carries a scroll padding of the top bar's height and `--bottom-bar-space`. A field drawn inside a larger shape (the top bar's search, the uploader's drop zone) shows the focus on that shape. `test/system/focus_visibility_test.rb` holds both.
- ARIA attributes go in an `aria:` hash, and a state is the word `"true"` or `"false"`, which a lint rule enforces. A Rails builder writes an `aria_label:` key as it is, and Phlex writes a `true` attribute bare, which hides nothing.
- Every icon is `Components::Icon`. It is hidden from screen readers, because the words beside it already say what it means. An icon that stands for words of its own, such as a low-stock warning, is given a label and announced as an image.
- A control whose text is a count (the bell) is named by words and that count.
- A landmark that a page can hold twice, such as a pager (`Components::Pagination`'s `list:`), is named after the list it belongs to. A heading or a name shown in short (a calendar day's "WED 21") is read out whole from words for screen readers alone, and a word in another language (a language's own name) says so with `lang`.

## The installed app

The app installs as a PWA. The service worker shows an offline page and push notifications, and never caches the app's pages or data. It keeps the offline page in every language and shows the one the browser reads first; the page stands at the address that failed, so trying again asks for that page. The push notifications use Web Push (VAPID): each person turns them on per device, and every notification reaches their devices. Tapping one marks it read under the bell, then opens what it is about; a link to a notification only leads there and changes nothing. A push service that is busy or can't be reached is tried again, later each time; any other refusal is reported and the push dropped, and a device refused every push for a month is forgotten, as one the service says is gone.

The bell's list loads the first time it opens and again each time it opens after that, so its rows say what has been read since and how long ago; new rows arrive live while it is open, and older ones come a page at a time.

A notification keeps what happened: its kind, what it is about, and the data its words need. What it says (`Notifications::Wording`) and where it leads (`Notifications::Destination`, through `polymorphic_path`, with a `resolve` line in the routes for a thing with no page of its own) are worked out where it is shown, in the reader's language, so the bell's row, the toast and the push say the same thing; they are presenters (`app/presenters`), not components, since they render no HTML.
